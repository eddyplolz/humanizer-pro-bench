**State University Announces Free Tuition Program for Low-Income Students**

State University announced Tuesday that it will offer free tuition to undergraduate students from low-income families, a move school leaders say is aimed at expanding access to higher education and reducing student debt.

Beginning in the fall semester next year, students whose families earn $75,000 or less annually will qualify for the new tuition-free program, according to university officials. The initiative will cover the full cost of tuition after federal and state grants are applied, though students may still be responsible for expenses such as housing, meals, and books.

University President Maria Ellis called the plan a “transformational investment” in students and the broader community.

“No talented student should feel that a college education is out of reach because of cost,” Ellis said at a campus news conference. “This program sends a clear message: if you have the ability and the drive to succeed, we will do everything we can to make that possible.”

The university said the program is expected to benefit nearly 4,000 current and incoming students each year. Funding will come from a combination of endowment earnings, private donations, and a reallocation of institutional financial aid resources. Officials said the school recently completed a fundraising campaign that exceeded its goals, helping make the initiative possible.

The announcement comes as colleges and universities across the country face growing pressure to address affordability. Tuition costs have climbed steadily over the past decade, leaving many students and families worried about taking on significant debt. Higher education advocates have increasingly pushed institutions with strong financial resources to expand need-based aid.

Students on campus welcomed the news, calling it a major step toward easing financial stress. “This could change everything for families like mine,” said sophomore Jasmine Carter, who works part-time while attending classes. “A lot of students are balancing school with jobs just to stay enrolled.”

University administrators said eligibility will be based on financial information submitted through standard aid applications. The school also plans to increase advising and academic support services for students participating in the program, with the goal of improving retention and graduation rates.

Some policy experts praised the initiative but noted that covering tuition alone may not eliminate all barriers. Living expenses often make up a substantial share of college costs, especially for students from lower-income households.

Even so, education analysts described the announcement as a significant commitment at a time when affordability remains one of the biggest challenges in higher education.

Applications for the tuition-free program will open alongside the university’s regular admissions and financial aid process later this year. Officials said additional details, including income verification requirements and renewal criteria, will be published on the university’s website in the coming weeks.
