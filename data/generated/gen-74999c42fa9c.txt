# Regional Airline Cancels Dozens of Flights as Pilot Shortage Deepens

**Skyline Air grounds routes serving smaller cities, leaving thousands of travelers scrambling**

*By Staff Reporter*

Regional carrier Skyline Air announced Monday that it will cancel roughly 120 flights over the next six weeks, citing a shortage of qualified pilots that the company says has reached a critical point.

The cancellations will affect service to eight mid-sized and rural markets, including Cedar Falls, Marlow County, and Pine Harbor. Three of those communities will lose daily service entirely until at least mid-March, according to a company statement.

"We deeply regret the disruption to our customers and the communities we serve," said Dana Whitfield, Skyline Air's chief operating officer. "We have exhausted every option to cover these routes safely and within federal duty-time regulations. Without enough pilots, we cannot operate the schedule we promised."

The airline said it has lost nearly 15 percent of its pilot workforce over the past year, as many have left for major carriers offering higher salaries, signing bonuses, and faster paths to international routes. Skyline currently employs about 340 pilots, down from 400 last January.

Industry analysts say Skyline's troubles reflect a wider national problem. A wave of retirements among veteran pilots, combined with a federal requirement that airline captains log 1,500 flight hours, has narrowed the pipeline of new hires. Regional airlines, which often serve as training grounds for future major-carrier pilots, have been hit hardest.

"Regionals are the farm system of the industry," said Marcus Tran, an aviation consultant. "When the big airlines hire aggressively, the regionals bleed talent. Then the small towns that depend on them lose air service, and it's very hard to get it back."

For passengers, the impact has been immediate. At Cedar Falls Regional Airport on Monday, traveler Priya Natarajan said her flight to a family wedding was canceled with two days' notice. "I got a text and an email, and that was it," she said. "Now I'm looking at a five-hour drive to the nearest hub."

Local officials worry about longer-term consequences. Cedar Falls Mayor Tom Hendricks said reliable air service is vital for attracting employers and supporting local businesses. "Losing flights isn't just an inconvenience," he said. "It affects our economy."

Skyline said affected passengers will receive full refunds or be rebooked on partner airlines where possible. The company also announced a new retention package, including raises of up to 18 percent and a tuition-assistance program for aspiring pilots.

Whitfield said the airline hopes to restore full service by April but declined to guarantee a timeline.
