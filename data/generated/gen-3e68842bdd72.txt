# Wildfire Forces Thousands to Evacuate Near Cedar Ridge National Park

**CEDAR FALLS, Mont.** — A fast-moving wildfire tore through dry timber along the western edge of Cedar Ridge National Park on Tuesday, forcing the evacuation of more than 3,000 residents and prompting officials to close the park to all visitors.

The blaze, named the Hollow Creek Fire, was first reported shortly after 2 p.m. Monday and has since burned an estimated 14,000 acres. Fire officials said it was zero percent contained as of Wednesday morning.

"This fire is moving faster than anything we've seen in this area in a decade," said Incident Commander Dana Whitfield at a evening briefing. "Dry fuels, low humidity, and gusting winds are all working against us."

Mandatory evacuation orders were issued for the communities of Pine Hollow, Larkspur, and parts of Cedar Falls. An additional 2,000 residents in neighboring areas were told to be ready to leave on short notice. Sheriff's deputies went door to door overnight, and emergency alerts were sent to cell phones throughout the county.

An evacuation center opened at Cedar Falls High School, where the Red Cross provided cots, meals, and information. By midday, more than 400 people had checked in. Many arrived with pets, medications, and little else.

"We had about fifteen minutes to grab what we could," said Maria Delgado, a Larkspur resident who left with her two children and their dog. "I took photo albums, documents, and clothes. You just hope the house is still there when you get back."

Park Superintendent Tom Ellery said all campgrounds and trails had been cleared and that rangers had accounted for all registered backcountry visitors. "Visitor safety is our top priority," he said. "We are also working closely with firefighters to protect historic structures, including the Ridgeline Lodge, which dates to 1932."

More than 600 firefighters, supported by helicopters and air tankers, are battling the flames. Crews have focused on building containment lines near homes and protecting power infrastructure. Two firefighters suffered minor heat-related injuries, officials said. No civilian injuries or deaths have been reported.

Smoke has drifted across the region, and health officials issued air quality warnings for three counties, urging children, older adults, and people with respiratory conditions to stay indoors.

Meteorologists said a slight chance of rain may arrive late Friday, though thunderstorms could also bring dangerous lightning and erratic winds.

Governor Elaine Parks declared a state of emergency Tuesday, freeing up additional state resources. Officials have not determined the cause of the fire, and an investigation is underway.

Residents can find updates and shelter information on the county emergency management website.
