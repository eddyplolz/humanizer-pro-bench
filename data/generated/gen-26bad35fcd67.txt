**Only Grocery Store in Maple Ridge to Close, Leaving Residents Facing Long Trips for Basics**

**MAPLE RIDGE, June 12** — Residents of Maple Ridge are preparing for a major change after the town’s only grocery store announced it will close at the end of the month, raising concerns about access to food, medicine and other household essentials.

Maple Ridge Market, which has served the town for more than three decades, will shut its doors on June 30, according to owner Daniel Harper. The store has been the community’s primary source for groceries, fresh produce, dairy products and over-the-counter medications. Its closure means residents will need to travel nearly 25 miles to the next nearest supermarket.

Harper said a combination of rising costs, supply chain challenges and declining sales made it impossible to continue operating.

“We’ve done everything we could to keep the store open,” Harper said in an interview Tuesday. “But higher wholesale prices, utility bills, and the difficulty of competing with larger chains have taken a real toll. This wasn’t an easy decision.”

For many in Maple Ridge, population 1,800, the market was more than a place to shop. It also served as an informal gathering spot where neighbors caught up on local news and checked in on one another. Residents say the loss will be especially hard on older adults, low-income families and people without reliable transportation.

“This store is a lifeline,” said longtime resident Martha Jennings, 72. “Not everyone can just drive to the city whenever they need milk or bread. A lot of people here depend on this place.”

Town officials say they are exploring temporary solutions, including partnering with nearby food banks, organizing volunteer ride programs and encouraging mobile food vendors to stop in Maple Ridge regularly. Mayor Carla Ruiz called the closure “a serious challenge” and said the town is already in discussions with regional agencies and local nonprofits.

“We know this will create hardship for many families,” Ruiz said. “Our priority is making sure no one in Maple Ridge is left without access to basic necessities.”

The closure also means the loss of 12 jobs, a significant blow in a town where employment opportunities are limited. Harper said he hopes some employees will be able to find work in neighboring communities, though he acknowledged the transition will be difficult.

Residents have started circulating petitions and discussing cooperative ownership models in hopes of eventually reopening a grocery option in town. Some are calling for state assistance to address what advocates describe as a growing “rural food access” problem affecting small communities across the region.

Until then, shelves at Maple Ridge Market are beginning to thin as customers make what may be their final trips through the aisles. For many in town, the store’s closing marks not just the loss of a business, but the end of a familiar part of daily life.
