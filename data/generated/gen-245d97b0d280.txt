**Record Turnout Marks Local School-Board Election**

Voters turned out in record numbers Tuesday for the Riverton School Board election, signaling unusually high public interest in an office that often attracts little attention outside education circles.

According to the Riverton County Elections Office, more than 18,400 ballots were cast in the race, representing roughly 46 percent of registered voters in the district. Officials said that was the highest turnout ever recorded for a stand-alone school-board election in the community, surpassing the previous mark set in 2017 by nearly 5,000 votes.

Election workers reported steady lines throughout the day at polling sites across the district, with especially heavy traffic in the morning and again after work hours. Early voting and absentee ballots also climbed sharply, contributing to the unprecedented participation.

“This is the kind of civic engagement every local government hopes to see,” said County Elections Director Marisol Vega. “School-board elections typically bring in a smaller slice of the electorate, so this level of turnout is remarkable.”

The heightened interest followed months of public debate over school funding, classroom overcrowding, campus safety, and the future direction of the district’s curriculum. Candidate forums drew large audiences, and community groups on all sides mounted extensive outreach campaigns urging residents to vote.

In the race for three open seats, incumbent Daniel Harper retained his position, while newcomers Alicia Gomez and Trent Wallace appeared headed for victory based on unofficial returns released Tuesday night. Final certified results are expected later this week, though the margins were wide enough that observers said the outcome was unlikely to change.

Supporters of the winning candidates celebrated what they called a clear message from voters that the district needs both stability and fresh ideas. “People care deeply about what happens in our schools,” Gomez told supporters at a gathering Tuesday evening. “Tonight’s turnout shows this community is paying attention and wants a voice in the decisions affecting students and families.”

Not everyone agreed on the meaning of the results, but many residents said the sheer number of voters was encouraging. Parents, teachers, and retirees interviewed outside polling sites described a shared belief that the school board has an outsized impact on daily life, from academic standards to bus routes and extracurricular programs.

Superintendent Karen Liu said the election demonstrated broad public investment in the district’s future. “Regardless of who people supported, it is heartening to see so many residents participate,” Liu said. “That kind of involvement strengthens public education.”

Local analysts said the turnout could reshape future elections, proving that school-board races can mobilize voters when issues feel immediate and personal. For Riverton, Tuesday’s results delivered not only new leadership but also a notable show of democratic engagement.
