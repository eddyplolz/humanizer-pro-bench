# Record Turnout Marks Tuesday's School Board Election in Maplewood County

**Voters surge to the polls in the district's highest-participation off-year race in decades**

MAPLEWOOD — Residents of Maplewood County turned out in historic numbers Tuesday to elect three members to the school board, with election officials reporting that 41 percent of registered voters cast ballots. That is more than double the turnout of the last school board election and the highest for a stand-alone local race in the county's recorded history.

The County Elections Office said 38,214 of the district's 93,200 registered voters participated, compared with roughly 17 percent two years ago. Several precincts reported lines that stretched out the door during the after-work rush, and the polls at Lincoln Elementary stayed open 25 minutes past the scheduled 7 p.m. closing time to accommodate those already waiting.

"We prepared for a busy day, but nothing like this," said Dolores Whitfield, the county's elections director. "By noon we had already surpassed the total in-person turnout from the last cycle."

Observers attributed the surge to a contentious campaign focused on a proposed budget shortfall, school consolidation plans, and debates over curriculum and class sizes. Six candidates competed for three open seats, and the race drew unusually large campaign spending for a local contest, along with a series of packed candidate forums.

Parent groups on both sides of the consolidation debate organized door-knocking efforts and carpools to the polls. Student volunteers from Maplewood High School also ran a registration drive in the fall that added nearly 1,800 new voters to the rolls.

"I've lived here 30 years and never voted in a school board race until this year," said Marcus Delgado, 52, a father of two who waited about 20 minutes to vote at Lincoln Elementary. "Decisions about my kids' classrooms are being made, and I wanted a say."

Others said the high stakes brought them out. "Closing a school affects the whole neighborhood, not just the families who go there," said Ruth Ann Kessler, a retired teacher.

Preliminary results show incumbent Priya Raman leading the field, followed by newcomers Thomas Berglund and Angela Ortiz, who currently hold the other two winning positions. The margin between third and fourth place was fewer than 400 votes, and the elections office said final results will not be certified until provisional and mail-in ballots are counted next week.

Whitfield said her office would review the day's operations to prepare for future high-turnout elections. "This is a good problem to have," she said. "We hope it's a sign of lasting engagement."

The new board members will be sworn in at the first meeting of the new year.
