**Tbilisi** is the capital and largest city of **Georgia**, located on the banks of the **Mtkvari River** in the eastern part of the country. It is the political, economic, and cultural center of Georgia, with a population of over one million people. Owing to its position at the crossroads of Europe and Asia, Tbilisi has historically been an important hub for trade, diplomacy, and cultural exchange.

The city’s name is derived from the Georgian word **“tbili”**, meaning “warm,” a reference to the area’s natural sulfur hot springs. According to tradition, Tbilisi was founded in the 5th century by **King Vakhtang I Gorgasali** of Iberia after he discovered the springs while hunting. Archaeological evidence, however, indicates settlement in the area earlier than this. Tbilisi became the capital of the eastern Georgian kingdom and developed into a prominent urban center along regional trade routes.

Throughout its history, Tbilisi was frequently contested by regional powers, including Persians, Arabs, Byzantines, Seljuks, Mongols, and Ottomans. It was also influenced by Russian imperial expansion in the 19th century, becoming an important administrative center of the **Russian Empire** in the Caucasus. After brief periods of independence and Soviet rule, Tbilisi became the capital of independent Georgia in 1991 following the dissolution of the **Soviet Union**.

Tbilisi is noted for its diverse architecture, which reflects the city’s layered history. Its **Old Town** features narrow streets, traditional wooden balconies, churches, mosques, and bathhouses, while later districts include neoclassical, Art Nouveau, Soviet modernist, and contemporary buildings. Important landmarks include **Narikala Fortress**, **Metekhi Church**, **Sameba Cathedral**, **Rustaveli Avenue**, and the historic sulfur baths of **Abanotubani**.

The city is a major center of education, arts, and science in Georgia. It is home to **Tbilisi State University**, one of the oldest universities in the Caucasus, as well as museums, theaters, galleries, and research institutions. The **Georgian National Museum**, the **Shota Rustaveli Theatre**, and the **Tbilisi Opera and Ballet Theatre** are among its leading cultural institutions.

Tbilisi has a diversified economy based on services, finance, tourism, transportation, and industry. It also serves as the country’s main transportation hub, with an international airport, railway connections, and a metro system. In recent decades, the city has undergone extensive redevelopment and modernization while retaining much of its historic character.

Today, Tbilisi is recognized for its multicultural heritage, vibrant public life, and distinctive urban landscape. It remains one of the most important cities in the South Caucasus and a symbol of Georgian statehood and identity.
