**Ferry Service Resumes After Two-Year Suspension, Restoring Vital Link for Coastal Communities**

A long-awaited ferry service resumed operations on সোমবার, ending a two-year suspension that had disrupted travel, trade, and daily life for thousands of residents along the coast.

The route, which connects Harbor Point with Seaview Island, had been halted in 2024 following extensive storm damage to port infrastructure and subsequent safety upgrades. Its return was welcomed by commuters, business owners, and local officials who said the service is essential to the region’s economy and social life.

The first sailing departed shortly after 7 a.m., carrying passengers, vehicles, and freight across the bay for the first time in nearly 24 months. At both terminals, small crowds gathered to watch the vessel’s departure and arrival, with many passengers describing the resumption as a sign of recovery for the area.

“This ferry is more than transportation — it’s a lifeline,” said Mayor Elena Brooks at a reopening ceremony at Harbor Point. “For two years, families, workers, and businesses have had to rely on longer, more expensive alternatives. Today marks an important step forward.”

During the suspension, travelers were forced to take a detour of more than 80 miles by road or depend on limited private boat services. Local businesses, particularly tourism operators, restaurants, and suppliers, said the interruption led to higher costs and fewer visitors during peak seasons.

Port authorities said significant repairs and modernization work had been completed at both terminals before the route reopened. Improvements included reinforced docking facilities, upgraded passenger waiting areas, and new navigation and safety systems designed to make the crossing more reliable in poor weather.

The ferry operator, Coastal Transit, said the service will initially run three round trips daily, with plans to expand to a full timetable by the summer if demand remains strong. Tickets went on sale last month, and the company reported “encouraging” early bookings from both residents and tourists.

“We know how important this connection is,” said Coastal Transit spokesperson Daniel Reed. “Over the past two years, our focus has been on rebuilding safely and making sure passengers can return with confidence.”

Passengers on the first crossing expressed relief at the route’s return. “This cuts my commute in half,” said schoolteacher Maria Chen, who travels to Seaview Island for work. “It’s going to make a huge difference, not just for me, but for everyone who depends on getting back and forth every day.”

Local officials said the reopening could provide a timely boost ahead of the busy holiday season, when visitor numbers typically rise. While some residents called for additional sailings and lower fares, many said they were simply glad to see the ferry back in service.

For the coastal communities on both sides of the bay, the return of the ferry represents not only renewed convenience, but also a sense of normalcy after two difficult years.
