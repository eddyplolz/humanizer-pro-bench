# Tbilisi

**Tbilisi** (Georgian: თბილისი), historically known as **Tiflis**, is the capital and largest city of Georgia. It lies on the banks of the Mtkvari (Kura) River and had a population of approximately 1.2 million in the early 2020s, representing roughly a third of the country's inhabitants. The city is a major cultural, economic, and transportation hub in the South Caucasus.

## History

According to tradition, King Vakhtang I Gorgasali of Iberia founded the city in the 5th century AD after discovering warm sulfur springs while hunting. The name derives from the Old Georgian *tbili*, meaning "warm." His successors moved the royal seat to Tbilisi, and the city grew in importance despite repeated invasions by Persians, Byzantines, Arabs, Mongols, and Turkic dynasties.

Tbilisi flourished during the Georgian Golden Age under King David IV and Queen Tamar in the 12th and early 13th centuries, although it was later sacked several times, notably by Timur and by the Persian ruler Agha Mohammad Khan in 1795. Georgia was annexed by the Russian Empire in the early 19th century, and Tbilisi became the administrative center of the Caucasus Viceroyalty. After a brief independence from 1918 to 1921, the city became the capital of the Georgian Soviet Socialist Republic. Georgia regained independence in 1991, and Tbilisi has since experienced political upheaval, including the Rose Revolution of 2003, as well as considerable modernization.

## Geography and climate

The city occupies a valley surrounded by mountains and hills, at an elevation of roughly 400–770 meters above sea level. Tbilisi has a transitional climate between humid subtropical and continental, with hot summers and cool winters.

## Culture and architecture

Tbilisi is known for its eclectic architecture, which blends medieval, neoclassical, Art Nouveau, Soviet, and contemporary styles. The Old Town (Dzveli Tbilisi) features narrow streets, carved wooden balconies, and the sulfur baths of Abanotubani. Prominent landmarks include the Narikala Fortress, the Metekhi Church, the Sioni Cathedral, the Holy Trinity Cathedral (Sameba), and the modern Bridge of Peace. The Kartlis Deda ("Mother of Georgia") statue overlooks the city from Sololaki Ridge.

The city hosts numerous theaters, museums, and festivals, including the Georgian National Museum and the Rustaveli Theatre. Georgian cuisine, including *khachapuri*, *khinkali*, and local wines, is a significant part of the city's identity. Its nightlife and electronic music scene have also gained international attention.

## Economy and transport

Tbilisi is the country's financial and commercial center, with major sectors including services, tourism, trade, and manufacturing. The Tbilisi Metro, opened in 1966, is the city's main rapid transit system, supplemented by buses, minibuses, and a cable car. Tbilisi International Airport is the principal gateway to the country.
