# Artemisia Gentileschi

**Artemisia Gentileschi** (8 July 1593 – c. 1656) was an Italian Baroque painter, widely regarded as one of the most accomplished artists of her generation. Working in the tradition of Caravaggio, she was known for her dramatic compositions, naturalistic style, and powerful depictions of heroic women from myth, scripture, and history. She was the first woman admitted to the Accademia delle Arti del Disegno in Florence.

## Early life

Artemisia was born in Rome, the eldest child of the painter Orazio Gentileschi and Prudentia Montone. Her mother died when Artemisia was about twelve. Trained in her father's workshop, she learned drawing, color mixing, and painting techniques. Because women were barred from formal academies, her education was largely confined to the studio. Her earliest signed work, *Susanna and the Elders* (1610), demonstrates remarkable skill for a seventeen-year-old.

## Trial of Agostino Tassi

In 1611, Artemisia was raped by Agostino Tassi, a painter and colleague of her father who had been hired to teach her perspective. Orazio brought charges in 1612, and a seven-month trial followed. Artemisia was reportedly examined under torture with thumbscrews to verify her testimony. Tassi was convicted, though his sentence of exile from Rome was not enforced for long. Surviving trial transcripts are a rare record of a woman's testimony from this period.

## Career

Shortly after the trial, Artemisia married the Florentine artist Pierantonio Stiattesi and moved to Florence. There she enjoyed considerable success, gaining the patronage of Cosimo II de' Medici and forming a friendship with Galileo Galilei. In 1616 she became a member of the Accademia delle Arti del Disegno. Works from this period include *Judith Slaying Holofernes* (c. 1614–20) and *Self-Portrait as a Lute Player*.

Financial difficulties and debts led her to return to Rome in the 1620s, and she later worked in Venice and Naples. In 1638 she joined her father at the court of Charles I in England, and after Orazio's death in 1639 she completed some of his commissions. She spent much of her later career in Naples, where she collaborated with other painters and received prominent commissions.

## Style and themes

Gentileschi's paintings are marked by strong chiaroscuro, vivid color, and emotional intensity. She frequently depicted women such as Judith, Susanna, Esther, Bathsheba, and Cleopatra. Her heroines are physically strong and psychologically complex, a contrast to the more passive depictions common among male contemporaries.

## Legacy

After her death, Gentileschi was largely overlooked for centuries. In the 20th century, art historians, notably feminist scholars, rediscovered her work and reassessed her significance. Her life and art have since inspired books, plays, and films, and major exhibitions, including one at the National Gallery, London, in 2020, have cemented her reputation as a leading Baroque master.
