**Relentless Heat Wave Pushes State Power Grid to the Brink**

A prolonged heat wave blanketing the state this week has placed intense strain on the power grid, prompting energy officials to urge residents and businesses to conserve electricity during peak evening hours as air conditioners run nonstop across millions of homes.

Temperatures climbed into the upper 90s and above 100 degrees in several inland communities for a third consecutive day, while overnight lows remained unusually warm, offering little relief and keeping cooling demand elevated around the clock. Grid operators said electricity use surged sharply Monday and Tuesday, approaching seasonal records as households sought refuge from dangerous heat.

“We are seeing exceptionally high demand driven by sustained extreme temperatures,” the state’s independent grid operator said in a statement. “At this time, the system remains stable, but conservation is critical to reduce stress on the grid during peak hours.”

Officials asked consumers to limit nonessential electricity use between late afternoon and early evening, when solar generation begins to decline but air-conditioning demand remains high. Recommended measures included raising thermostats a few degrees, delaying use of large appliances such as dishwashers and washing machines, and turning off unnecessary lights.

The heat wave also tested power infrastructure as utilities worked to prevent outages caused by overtaxed equipment. In some areas, transformers and local distribution lines faced increased pressure, raising the possibility of isolated blackouts even if the broader grid continued operating normally. Utility crews were placed on heightened alert to respond to equipment failures.

Emergency management officials warned that any loss of electricity could be especially dangerous for vulnerable residents, including older adults, young children, and people with medical conditions. Cities and counties opened cooling centers at libraries, community centers, and other public buildings, while health agencies urged people to stay hydrated, avoid strenuous outdoor activity, and check on neighbors.

The current heat wave comes as utilities and policymakers confront growing concerns about the resilience of the state’s energy system in the face of more frequent extreme weather. Climate scientists have long warned that rising temperatures are increasing the likelihood of prolonged periods of intense heat, which can drive record electricity demand while also threatening transmission equipment and reducing the efficiency of some power plants.

Businesses dependent on uninterrupted electricity, including manufacturers and food retailers, said they were watching conditions closely. Some large industrial customers voluntarily reduced consumption under demand-response programs designed to ease grid pressure.

Forecasters said the hottest conditions are expected to persist through at least midweek before a modest cooldown arrives. Until then, officials said, avoiding widespread outages may depend in part on whether residents heed calls to conserve.

“This is a moment when small actions taken by millions of people can make a meaningful difference,” the grid operator said.
