# Ferry Service Returns to Harbor Island After Two-Year Absence

**PORT MARLOWE —** Cheers and a chorus of horns greeted the *Marguerite* on Monday morning as she pulled into Port Marlowe, marking the return of passenger ferry service between the mainland and Harbor Island after a two-year suspension.

More than 300 residents gathered along the waterfront to watch the 140-foot vessel complete its first scheduled crossing since service was halted in the spring of 2022. Some waved homemade banners. Others simply stood quietly, hands over their mouths, as the ferry's ramp lowered onto the dock.

"I've waited a long time to see that boat come in," said Eleanor Briggs, 78, who has lived on the island for six decades. "Two years of arranging rides on fishing boats and begging favors from neighbors. It's been exhausting."

The ferry line was suspended after an inspection revealed structural corrosion in the aging vessel that served the route for nearly 40 years. Repeated delays in securing replacement funding, compounded by supply chain problems and a shipyard backlog, stretched what officials initially projected as a six-month interruption into two years.

The *Marguerite*, purchased last year from a retired Scandinavian route and refitted at a cost of $14.2 million, can carry 180 passengers and 22 vehicles. The state transportation department covered roughly 70 percent of the expense, with the remainder coming from county funds and a federal rural transit grant.

Transportation Director Alan Pruitt called the launch "a long-overdue lifeline."

"This isn't just a boat," Pruitt said at the dockside ceremony. "It's how people get to work, how kids get to school, how patients reach the hospital. Its absence was felt in every part of island life."

The suspension took a visible toll. Harbor Island's population dropped an estimated 11 percent, according to county figures, and at least four small businesses closed. Tourism, the island's economic backbone, fell by more than half in the first year.

Local innkeeper Tomás Reyes said he nearly sold his business. "We had maybe a dozen bookings all last summer," he said. "Now my phone hasn't stopped ringing. I've got reservations through September."

The ferry will operate six round trips daily during the summer season, with a reduced schedule in winter. Fares are set at $12 for adult passengers and $38 for standard vehicles, with discounted rates for island residents.

Captain Dana Whitfield, who piloted the inaugural crossing, said the moment was meaningful for the crew as well. "We could see people lining the shore from a mile out," she said. "It gave me chills."

The next departure was scheduled for 11:30 a.m. It sold out within the hour.
