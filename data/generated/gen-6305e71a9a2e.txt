# Council Approves Citywide Bike-Lane Network in Split Vote After Marathon Meeting

**Plan passes 5-4 following five hours of public testimony; construction to begin in spring**

RIVERTON — After a contentious five-hour session that stretched past midnight, the Riverton City Council voted 5-4 Tuesday night to approve a $38 million network of protected bike lanes spanning 42 miles of city streets.

The plan, known as the Riverton Connects Initiative, will build physical barriers between cyclists and traffic along major corridors, including Main Street, Delancey Avenue, and the Harbor Road waterfront. Construction is scheduled to begin in April and conclude within four years.

More than 120 residents signed up to speak, filling the council chamber and an overflow room. Supporters wore green stickers reading "Safe Streets Now," while opponents carried signs warning of lost parking and business disruption.

"My daughter bikes to school, and every morning I hold my breath," said Maria Okafor, a nurse who lives on the east side. "We have lost three cyclists on these roads in five years. This is not abstract for us."

Opponents argued the plan would hurt small businesses by removing roughly 600 on-street parking spaces. Tom Hendricks, who owns a hardware store on Main Street, said his customers need to load heavy materials directly into their vehicles.

"I'm not against bikes," Hendricks told the council. "I'm against being told my business has to absorb the cost of an experiment."

Councilmember Dana Whitfield, who sponsored the measure, said the city had adjusted the proposal in response to concerns. Revisions included preserving loading zones near commercial districts and adding 250 off-street parking spaces in underused municipal lots.

"No plan will satisfy everyone," Whitfield said before the vote. "But doing nothing has a cost too, and we have been paying it in injuries and congestion."

Councilmember Robert Vance, who led the opposition, criticized the price tag and the pace of the rollout. "We are committing tens of millions of dollars while our water mains are failing," he said. "Residents deserve better prioritization."

An amendment from Vance that would have required a one-year pilot program before full approval failed 5-4, with the same members on each side.

Roughly 60 percent of the funding will come from state and federal transportation grants, with the remainder drawn from a municipal bond approved by voters in 2021.

Mayor Elaine Cho said she would sign the ordinance this week. The city will hold a series of neighborhood meetings in February to finalize design details for each corridor.

Outside City Hall after the vote, reactions remained sharply divided. "It's a start," said Okafor. Hendricks was blunter: "This isn't over."
