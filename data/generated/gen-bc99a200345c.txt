**Regional Airline Cancels Dozens of Flights Amid Pilot Shortage**

A regional airline has cancelled dozens of flights this week, citing an ongoing shortage of qualified pilots that has disrupted schedules and left passengers scrambling to make alternative travel arrangements.

The carrier, which operates short-haul routes connecting smaller cities to major hubs, said the cancellations were necessary to maintain safety and comply with federal crew-rest and staffing requirements. Airline officials said the decision was made after staffing levels fell below what was needed to operate the full schedule reliably.

“We regret the inconvenience to our customers, but safety remains our top priority,” the airline said in a statement released Tuesday. “Like many regional carriers across the industry, we are facing significant challenges in recruiting and retaining pilots.”

The cancellations affected routes in several states, with some passengers learning only hours before departure that their flights had been pulled from the schedule. At several regional airports, travelers reported long lines at customer service desks as airline staff worked to rebook customers on later flights or arrange ground transportation.

“I was supposed to fly out this morning for a business meeting, and now the next available flight isn’t until tomorrow,” said one passenger at a regional airport. “It’s frustrating because there weren’t many backup options.”

Industry analysts say pilot shortages have become an increasingly serious problem for regional airlines, which often serve as a training ground for aviators before they move to larger, better-paying mainline carriers. Regional airlines have struggled to compete on wages and quality of life, especially as demand for air travel has rebounded and larger airlines have expanded hiring.

The issue has also been compounded by mandatory retirement rules, higher training costs, and stricter experience requirements for new pilots. Together, those factors have narrowed the pipeline of available crew members, particularly for smaller carriers operating in less profitable markets.

Aviation consultant Mark Ellison said regional airlines are often hit hardest because they have fewer staffing reserves. “When a major airline loses a few pilots, it can sometimes absorb that disruption,” he said. “For a regional carrier, even a small staffing gap can force widespread cancellations.”

The airline said it is offering travel waivers and refunds to affected customers and is working to restore normal operations as quickly as possible. Company officials also said hiring efforts are underway, including recruitment bonuses and expanded training partnerships.

For communities that rely on regional air service, the disruptions underscore a broader concern about the stability of air links to smaller markets. Local business leaders and airport officials warn that prolonged service reductions could hurt tourism, business travel, and economic activity.

For now, passengers are being advised to check flight statuses before heading to the airport, as further schedule changes remain possible in the coming days.
