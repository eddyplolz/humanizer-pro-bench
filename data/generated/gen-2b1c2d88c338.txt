# Transit Strike Halts Buses and Trains, Leaving Thousands of Commuters Stranded

**Union rejects final offer as talks collapse overnight; city braces for second day of disruption**

Hundreds of thousands of commuters scrambled for alternatives Monday after public-transit workers walked off the job at dawn, shutting down bus and rail service across the metropolitan region.

The Metro Transit Workers Union, which represents roughly 8,200 drivers, mechanics, and station staff, began the strike at 4 a.m. after contract negotiations with the Regional Transit Authority broke down late Sunday. It is the first systemwide work stoppage in 14 years.

"We didn't want to be here," said union president Dolores Reyes, speaking from a picket line outside the Eastside bus depot. "But workers who keep this city moving can't be asked to fall behind on rent while costs rise every year."

The union is seeking a 9 percent wage increase over three years, stronger protections against assaults on drivers, and limits on mandatory overtime. Transit officials countered with a 5 percent raise and said they could not meet the remaining demands without raising fares or cutting routes.

"Our offer was fair and fiscally responsible," Authority spokesperson Martin Vail said in a statement. "We remain at the table and urge the union to return to negotiations so riders can get back to their lives."

The impact was immediate. Highways clogged before sunrise, with traffic officials reporting delays up to 70 percent longer than normal. Ride-share prices surged, and bike-share stations in the downtown core were emptied by 8 a.m. Several major employers encouraged staff to work from home.

At Central Station, normally packed with morning travelers, a handful of people stood reading posted closure notices. Marcus Hill, a hospital cafeteria worker, said he had walked two miles before finding a friend to drive him the rest of the way.

"I support the drivers, I really do," Hill said. "But I can't afford to miss shifts either. I'm stuck in the middle."

Schools reported higher-than-usual absences, and the district said it would keep yellow school buses running, since those are operated by a separate contractor. Paratransit service for riders with disabilities is operating at limited capacity under an emergency agreement, though advocates say wait times have stretched beyond two hours.

Mayor Elaine Cho called on both sides to resume talks immediately and offered city staff as mediators. "Every day this continues costs working families and local businesses," she said.

Economists estimate the shutdown could cost the regional economy $30 million per day in lost productivity and sales.

No new negotiating session has been scheduled. The union said it will continue picketing until it receives a "serious proposal," while the Authority said contingency planning is underway for a prolonged stoppage.
