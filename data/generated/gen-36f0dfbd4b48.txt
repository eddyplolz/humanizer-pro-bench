**Artemisia Gentileschi** (1593 – c. 1656) was an Italian Baroque painter, now regarded as one of the most important artists of the seventeenth century. She was among the first women to achieve professional success in a field then dominated by men, and is especially noted for her powerful depictions of biblical and mythological heroines.

Born in Rome, Artemisia was the daughter of the painter **Orazio Gentileschi**, from whom she received her earliest artistic training. Her work was strongly influenced by **Caravaggio**, whose dramatic use of light and shadow, emotional intensity, and naturalism shaped her style. Despite restrictions on women’s artistic education at the time, Artemisia demonstrated exceptional skill at a young age. One of her earliest known works, **_Susanna and the Elders_** (1610), already shows her ability to convey psychological tension and female experience with unusual force.

A major event in her life was the 1612 trial of **Agostino Tassi**, a painter who raped her. The trial, which subjected Artemisia to public scrutiny and torture to verify her testimony, has become central to modern interpretations of her life and art. Soon afterward, she married the Florentine painter **Pierantonio Stiattesi** and moved to Florence, where her career flourished. In 1616 she became the first woman admitted to the **Accademia delle Arti del Disegno**, an important sign of her professional recognition.

During her years in Florence, and later in Rome, Venice, and Naples, Artemisia produced many of her most famous paintings. These include **_Judith Slaying Holofernes_**, **_Judith and Her Maidservant_**, **_Lucretia_**, and **_Self-Portrait as the Allegory of Painting_**. Her works often feature women from history, scripture, and legend at moments of crisis, courage, or revenge. Although such subjects were not unique in Baroque art, Artemisia’s treatment of them has been widely praised for its intensity, dignity, and emotional complexity.

Artemisia also worked for elite patrons across Italy and beyond. She spent time in Naples, which became an important center of her later career, and probably traveled to London in the late 1630s to join her father at the court of **Charles I of England**. There she may have contributed to court commissions before returning to Naples.

For many years after her death, Artemisia Gentileschi was overshadowed by her male contemporaries and often remembered chiefly in relation to her biography. Since the twentieth century, however, her reputation has grown significantly. Scholars have emphasized both her technical achievement and her originality within Baroque painting. She is now celebrated not only as a pioneering woman artist, but also as a major painter whose work stands alongside that of the leading masters of her age.
