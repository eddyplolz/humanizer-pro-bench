# Westbrook University Announces Free Tuition for Students from Low-Income Families

**New "Westbrook Promise" program will cover full tuition for families earning under $75,000 beginning next fall**

WESTBROOK, Ohio — Westbrook University announced Tuesday that it will eliminate tuition for undergraduate students from families earning less than $75,000 per year, a move administrators say will open the doors of higher education to thousands of students who might otherwise be priced out.

The program, called the Westbrook Promise, takes effect with the incoming class next fall and will apply to all admitted students who are Ohio residents and meet the income threshold. Students will remain eligible for four years as long as they maintain satisfactory academic progress.

"Talent is distributed equally across our communities, but opportunity is not," said university President Dr. Elena Marchetti at a press conference on campus. "No student who earns a place at Westbrook should have to turn it down because of the price tag. With this program, we are keeping a promise to the families in our region."

The university estimates that roughly 4,200 current and future students will qualify during the first year. The program is expected to cost approximately $38 million annually, funded primarily through a $400 million endowment campaign launched two years ago, along with reallocated institutional aid and state grants.

Tuition at Westbrook currently stands at $14,800 per year for in-state students. Housing, meals, and books are not covered under the new program, though officials said students may still qualify for additional need-based aid to help with those costs.

Student reaction on campus was enthusiastic. Junior Marcus Delgado, who works two part-time jobs to cover his expenses, said the announcement came as a surprise. "I've thought about dropping out more than once because of the loans I was piling up," he said. "Knowing that students after me won't have to go through that is a big deal."

Education advocates praised the decision but cautioned that tuition is only one part of college affordability. "Free tuition is a major step, but living costs often determine whether students finish," said Dana Whitfield, director of the Ohio College Access Network. "We hope Westbrook will continue to expand support in those areas."

University officials acknowledged the concern. Provost James Okafor said the school plans to study additional support for housing, food insecurity, and transportation over the next year.

Applications for the incoming class are open through March 1. Families can learn about eligibility and apply for the Westbrook Promise through the university's financial aid office or website.

The university also plans to host information sessions at high schools across the region this winter.
