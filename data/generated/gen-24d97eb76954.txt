**Wildfire Forces Evacuations Near Redwood Canyon National Park**

**REDWOOD COUNTY, Calif. —** A fast-moving wildfire burning near Redwood Canyon National Park forced mandatory evacuations Tuesday as firefighters battled dry conditions, gusty winds and rugged terrain that officials said were fueling the blaze.

The fire, dubbed the **Canyon Ridge Fire**, ignited late Monday afternoon about five miles east of the park’s southern boundary and had grown to an estimated **7,800 acres** by Tuesday evening, according to the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection. Containment remained low as crews worked to protect homes, campgrounds and critical infrastructure in nearby mountain communities.

Authorities ordered evacuations for residents in the small towns of **Pine Creek, Alder Grove and Millstone**, as well as several rural neighborhoods along Highway 86. An evacuation warning was also issued for portions of the park’s gateway communities, where visitors were urged to be prepared to leave at short notice.

“This fire is moving rapidly and conditions can change minute by minute,” said **Incident Commander Laura Mendes** during an evening briefing. “Our top priority is life safety — getting people out of harm’s way and giving firefighters the room they need to work.”

No deaths or serious injuries had been reported as of Tuesday night, though officials said several structures were threatened and at least two outbuildings had been destroyed. The cause of the fire remains under investigation.

A section of **Highway 86** was closed to non-emergency traffic, and park officials announced the temporary closure of the national park’s southern entrance, along with several backcountry trails and campgrounds. Rangers began escorting campers from affected areas early Tuesday, while smoke blanketed nearby valleys and reduced visibility across the region.

The National Weather Service issued a **red flag warning** for the area, citing low humidity, high temperatures and wind gusts that could exceed 30 mph. Fire officials said those conditions were expected to persist into Wednesday, complicating containment efforts.

More than **600 firefighters**, supported by air tankers, helicopters and bulldozers, were assigned to the incident. Crews were working overnight to cut containment lines and protect structures in evacuation zones.

At an evacuation center set up at **Redwood County High School**, displaced residents gathered with pets, medications and a few personal belongings. Volunteers handed out food, water and blankets as families waited for updates.

“We grabbed what we could and left,” said **Pine Creek resident Maria Lopez**, who evacuated with her husband and two children. “You could see the glow on the ridge, and the wind was just pushing it closer.”

Officials urged residents to monitor emergency alerts and avoid the area. “If you have been told to leave, leave now,” Mendes said. “Do not wait to see the fire.”
